Family mediation is a space for dialogue facilitated by a professional. It is intended in particular for parents who are separating or already separated and who wish to organize their child's life together.

## The mediator's role

The family mediator is a **neutral and impartial** third party. They do not take sides with either parent and do not decide for them. Their role is to facilitate exchanges so that the parents find solutions themselves.

## How does the first appointment go?

The process generally begins with an **information meeting**. The mediator explains the framework, the rules and how the sessions work. Each parent can then decide whether or not to continue.

The following sessions cover the topics chosen by the parents: residence schedule, holidays, child-related expenses, day-to-day communication.

## Confidentiality

What is said in mediation is confidential. This rule allows everyone to speak freely, without fearing that their words will be used afterwards.

## What mediation makes possible

- Restoring dialogue when exchanges have become difficult.
- Building practical agreements, adapted to the family.
- Drafting, if the parents wish, a written agreement that can be submitted to the judge for approval (homologation).

## Its limits

Mediation relies on both parents' willingness to engage in dialogue. It is not suitable for every situation, particularly in cases of violence: in that case, other protective measures exist and should be preferred.

## Where to find a mediation service?

Family mediation services are often supported by the CAF (Caisse d'allocations familiales, the family benefits office). The fee may depend on income: ask the service directly.

For official, up-to-date information, see [Service-Public.fr](https://www.service-public.fr/) (Family section) or [justice.fr](https://www.justice.fr/), the official French government websites, and the **Parenting guide** entry on mediation in your territory.
