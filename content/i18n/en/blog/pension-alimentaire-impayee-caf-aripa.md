Child support, which French law calls the **contribution to the child’s upkeep and education** (*pension alimentaire*), covers the child’s needs: food, housing, school, health, leisure. When it is no longer paid, or paid late, the child is the first to suffer. Here are the possible remedies, from the simplest to the most binding.

## First of all: an enforceable order

Most remedies require the amount of child support to be set by an **enforceable order** (*titre exécutoire*), that is, a document that allows payment to be demanded:

- a **judgment** of the family judge;
- a **divorce by mutual consent agreement** filed with a notary;
- an **agreement approved** by the judge;
- for unmarried parents who separate, an **agreement to which the Caf has given enforceable force**.

Without an enforceable order, a simple verbal agreement cannot be recovered. The guide [applying to the family judge](/guide/guyane/saisir-juge-affaires-familiales) explains how to obtain one.

## 1. Try the amicable route first

A forgotten payment, a job loss, a change of bank: non-payment is not always a refusal. A **written, dated message**, factual and without reproach, reminds the other parent of the amount due and suggests a date for settling it. If dialogue is difficult, [family mediation](/guide/guyane/mediation-familiale) can help. If the other parent genuinely can no longer pay, it is up to the judge to review the amount: child support that has been set remains due until it is changed.

## 2. The public child support service (ARIPA)

**ARIPA** (*Agence de recouvrement et d’intermédiation des pensions alimentaires*) is run by the **Caf** (and by the MSA for families under the agricultural scheme). It offers two services:

- **financial intermediation**: each month the Caf collects the child support from the paying parent and passes it on to the other parent. There is no longer any direct transfer between parents, which often eases relations. Since 2023, it has been set up automatically for most new decisions setting child support, unless both parents refuse or the judge decides otherwise;
- **recovery of arrears**: in case of non-payment, ARIPA can itself take steps to recover the sums due, up to the **last 24 months** of arrears.

Applications are made on [pension-alimentaire.caf.fr](https://www.pension-alimentaire.caf.fr) (in French), with your enforceable order. Our guide [what to do if the other parent does not pay child support](/guide/guyane/pension-alimentaire-aripa) lists the documents to prepare.

## 3. The family support allowance (ASF)

If child support is not paid, you may, under certain conditions, receive the **family support allowance** (*allocation de soutien familial*) while the Caf pursues recovery. It can also top up child support set below the ASF amount. Conditions and the current amount are on caf.fr; see also our guide [family support allowance](/guide/guyane/allocation-soutien-familial).

## 4. Enforcement procedures

If you do not go through ARIPA, or in addition to it, other routes exist:

- the **direct payment procedure**, handled by a judicial officer (*commissaire de justice*): the sums are taken directly from the paying parent’s employer or bank;
- **wage garnishment**, ordered by the judge;
- **recovery by the Public Treasury**, possible if one of the previous procedures has failed.

These procedures involve costs and delays: a lawyer or a free legal advice point (*point-justice*) can guide you. If your income is modest, [legal aid](/guide/guyane/aide-juridictionnelle) can cover all or part of the costs.

## 5. Filing a complaint for family abandonment

Failing to pay child support set by an enforceable order for **more than two months** is the offence of **family abandonment** (*abandon de famille*, article 227-3 of the French Criminal Code), punishable by two years’ imprisonment and a €15,000 fine. The complaint is filed with the police, the gendarmerie or in writing with the public prosecutor. It does not bring the money in by itself: it adds to recovery steps without replacing them.

## Overseas: what changes

The rules are the same as on the mainland, but the contacts are local:

- in **French Guiana, Martinique, Guadeloupe and Réunion**, applications are made to the Caf of your department, which runs ARIPA;
- in **Mayotte**, family benefits are handled by the **Caisse de sécurité sociale de Mayotte (CSSM)**: ask it which services are available.

Some parents overseas report long response times. Keep **a written record of every exchange** (date, subject, answer obtained). Without a response, a **written complaint**, then the **Caf’s administrative mediator**, are the official remedies.

If the other parent lives abroad, for example in Brazil or Suriname, recovery goes through international conventions. Contact your Caf or a lawyer.

## Keeping track of payments

For every remedy, you need to show **what was due and what was paid**. In Lien Parental, the **Expenses** area records payments received and shared costs, month by month, and the **vault** keeps the judgment and the Caf’s letters. The **court file** gathers these items if you have to go back before the judge.
