---
title: "Who can write a testimonial for me?"
category: "Testimonials"
updatedAt: "October 25, 2026"
---

In principle, any adult who has directly observed facts relating to you, your child or your life as a parent.

The most common witnesses:

- Extended family (grandparents, uncles, aunts, adult cousins).
- Close friends who regularly see you with your child.
- Neighbors who observe daily life.
- Teachers, activity leaders, healthcare professionals.
- Colleagues, babysitters, nannies.

Two important rules:

- The witness must describe facts they have seen or heard themselves, not things they were told.
- Minor children cannot write a testimonial in this context. Their views are heard, where appropriate, by the judge or by a professional.

A concrete example: the nanny who looks after your child three afternoons a week can testify to how regularly you come, the state your child is in on arrival and her exchanges with you. A friend who "heard that the other parent..." cannot validly write it.

Also be careful about relationships: a current partner or a child of the witness can be named, but the judge will weigh their neutrality. Mix profiles (family, school, neighborhood) for a stronger case.

To build an evidence strategy suited to your situation, consult your lawyer.
