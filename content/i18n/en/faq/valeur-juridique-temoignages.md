---
title: "Do testimonials have legal value?"
category: "Testimonials"
updatedAt: "October 25, 2026"
---

Yes, provided they follow the form required by French law (Article 202 of the Code de procédure civile, the French Code of Civil Procedure).

Lien Parental generates statements that include the required elements:

- Full identity of the witness (last name, first names, date and place of birth, address, occupation).
- Any family relationship or interest with you.
- A statement indicating that the attestation is intended to be produced in court.
- A statement acknowledging the criminal penalties for a false attestation.
- A copy of the witness's ID attached.
- Handwritten statement and signature.

Lien Parental guides your loved one step by step so that nothing is missing.

A concrete example: an incomplete attestation (without ID, without a handwritten statement) may be set aside by the judge. With Lien Parental's guided template, these omissions are avoided.

Two important nuances:

- Having a testimonial in the correct form does not guarantee it will be considered convincing. The judge freely assesses the weight of each element in the file.
- A false or favor testimonial exposes its author to a penalty under Article 441-7 of the Code pénal (the French Criminal Code).

To know which testimonials to produce and in what order, consult your lawyer.
