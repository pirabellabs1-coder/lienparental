---
title: "Do testimonials have legal value?"
category: "Testimonials"
updatedAt: "October 28, 2026"
---

A written testimonial can be produced before the family court judge if it follows the form required by French law (Article 202 of the Code de procédure civile, the French Code of Civil Procedure).

The witness journey in Lien Parental collects the elements this article asks for:

- Full identity of the witness (last name, first names, date and place of birth, address, occupation).
- Any family relationship, relationship by marriage, subordination, working relationship or interest with the parties.
- A statement, ticked by the witness, indicating that the attestation is made to be produced in court and that a false attestation exposes them to criminal penalties.
- A copy of the witness's official ID document, stored privately.
- Place and date, and a signature drawn on screen.

The attestation is written in French, for the court. If your loved one uses the app in English or Portuguese, a reading translation is shown so they know what they are certifying.

Important: the law provides that the attestation is written, dated and signed by hand by its author. A signature drawn on screen does not replace a handwritten signature if the court requires one. Your loved one can therefore download the pre-filled attestation, print it, date it and sign it by hand (with the words "Lu et approuvé", read and approved), then give it to you with the copy of their ID document. The official form Cerfa no. 11527*03 can also be used.

Two important nuances:

- Having a testimonial in the correct form does not guarantee it will be considered convincing. The judge freely assesses the weight of each element in the file.
- A false or favor testimonial exposes its author to a penalty under Article 441-7 of the Code pénal (the French Criminal Code).

To know which testimonials to produce and in what form, consult your lawyer.
