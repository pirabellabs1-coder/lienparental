---
title: "Which documents can I put in the vault?"
category: "Vault"
updatedAt: "October 25, 2026"
---

All the documents that are useful in your life as a separated parent, and more broadly for your family records.

The most common:

- Judgments, court orders, settlement agreements.
- Letters from lawyers, JAF summonses (from the juge aux affaires familiales, the French family court judge), official reports.
- School, after-school, health and activity invoices.
- CAF statements (Caisse d'allocations familiales, the family benefits office), child support, taxes.
- Health records (carnets de santé), medical prescriptions, school reports.
- Screenshots of messages (making sure to respect the legal framework for their use).
- Photos of the child at handovers, injuries, significant events.

A concrete example: a parent preparing for a JAF hearing gathers in the vault the last six school reports, the last two pediatric prescriptions and all the activity invoices for the year. They find everything with one search when their lawyer asks for it.

Accepted formats: PDF, JPG, PNG, HEIC, DOCX, and most common office formats.

What Lien Parental recommends you avoid:

- Other people's documents without their consent.
- Illegal content.
- Audio recordings made without the other parent's knowledge, whose admissibility is very strictly regulated.

If in doubt about what can be presented in proceedings, consult your lawyer.
